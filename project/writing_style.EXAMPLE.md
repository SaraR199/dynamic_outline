# Writing Style Guide

This document defines the prose style, narrative techniques, and writing principles for your book.

---

## Narrative Voice

### POV and Tense
- **Point of View:** Third Person Limited
- **Tense:** Past tense
- **Deep POV:** Yes - stay tightly in character's head, use their vocabulary and thought patterns

### Voice Characteristics
- Noir-influenced: Sharp, observational, slightly cynical
- Sensory-rich: Use all five senses to ground scenes
- Internal monologue: Character's thoughts in italics when needed for emphasis
- Pacing: Varies - tense scenes use short sentences, emotional scenes allow breathing room

---

## Prose Style

### Sentence Structure
- **Variety is key:** Mix short punchy sentences with longer flowing ones
- **Action scenes:** Short, sharp, present-feeling despite past tense
- **Emotional scenes:** Longer sentences that breathe and reflect
- **Avoid:** Purple prose, over-description, excessive adverbs

### Paragraph Length
- Short paragraphs for impact (one-liners for emphasis)
- Medium paragraphs for standard narrative
- Longer paragraphs only for complex internal processing or world-building info dumps (use sparingly)

### Vocabulary
- Contemporary but not overly casual
- Character-appropriate (detective uses cop jargon, fae use archaic phrases)
- Avoid modern slang that will date the text
- Magical terms should feel weighty and earned

---

## Showing vs. Telling

### Show Through:
- Body language and physical reactions
- Dialogue subtext (what's NOT said)
- Environmental details that reflect emotion
- Action and behavior

### Tell When:
- Conveying necessary backstory efficiently
- Summarizing time passage
- Delivering information POV character already knows
- Establishing stakes quickly

### Example of Showing:
❌ **Telling:** Thea was angry at Kyrian.

✅ **Showing:** Thea's jaw tightened. Her fingers curled into fists at her sides, nails biting into her palms. When she looked at Kyrian, her eyes were chips of winter ice.

---

## Dialogue Style

### General Principles
- **Realistic but polished:** How people actually talk, but edited for clarity
- **Subtext matters:** Characters often don't say what they mean directly
- **Unique voices:** Each character sounds distinct
- **Action beats:** Intersperse dialogue with character action/reaction
- **Avoid:** Excessive dialogue tags, on-the-nose exposition

### Dialogue Tags
- **Default:** "said" (invisible to reader)
- **Alternatives:** Use sparingly and only when they add meaning (whispered, growled, demanded)
- **Action beats instead:** Often better than tags
  - ✅ "I don't believe you." Thea crossed her arms.
  - ❌ "I don't believe you," Thea said disbelievingly.

### Example Dialogue:

```
"You can't just show up here," Thea said, not moving from the doorway.

Kyrian's smile didn't reach his eyes. "And yet, here I am."

"That's not an answer."

"You never did like ambiguity." He stepped closer, and she caught the scent of winter wind and pine. "Always wanting everything spelled out, categorized, solved."

"I'm a detective. It's what I do." She held her ground, even as every instinct screamed at her to retreat. Or advance. She couldn't tell which was worse.

"You're more than that now." His voice dropped. "Whether you accept it or not."
```

## Scene Structure

### Opening
- Start as late as possible (in media res when appropriate)
- Ground reader quickly: Who, where, what's happening
- Hook with conflict, question, or tension

### Middle
- Escalate tension or deepen emotion
- Use dialogue, action, and internal monologue in balance
- Reveal information through character discovery
- Maintain POV character's perspective rigorously

### Ending
- End on a beat that pulls forward (question, decision, cliffhanger)
- Avoid neat wrap-ups mid-story
- Create propulsion to next scene/chapter

---

## Pacing Techniques

### To Increase Pace:
- Short sentences and paragraphs
- More dialogue
- Active verbs
- Cut description to essentials
- Present-feeling prose despite past tense
- Sentence fragments for impact

### To Slow Pace:
- Longer sentences
- More internal monologue
- Sensory description
- Emotional processing
- World-building details
- Philosophical reflection

---

## Magic and World-Building Integration

### Don't Info-Dump
- Reveal world details through character interaction with world
- Use conflict to reveal rules (character tries something, discovers limitation)
- Trust reader to infer some details

### Magic Should Feel Magical
- Use sensory language (what does magic smell/taste/feel like?)
- Make it cost something (effort, pain, trade-off)
- Tie to emotion and character state

### Example:
❌ **Info-dump:** The mate bond was a magical connection between compatible fae that caused physical and emotional attraction and was considered unbreakable destiny.

✅ **Integrated:** The bond flared to life like a live wire touched to her chest. Thea gasped, her hand flying to her sternum where warmth bloomed and spread. Across the clearing, Kyrian's eyes widened—he felt it too. The air between them hummed with invisible current, trying to pull them together like magnets. This was the mate bond. The thing fae called destiny and Thea called a violation.

---

## Things to Avoid

- ❌ Over-explaining magic or world rules through exposition
- ❌ Characters thinking about things they already know for reader benefit
- ❌ Excessive filtering ("she saw," "she heard," "she felt")
- ❌ Melodrama or overwrought emotion
- ❌ Repetitive sentence structure
- ❌ Cliché phrases and descriptions
- ❌ Head-hopping within a scene (stay in one POV per scene)

---

## Things to Embrace

- ✅ Character voice coloring the narrative
- ✅ Specific sensory details that matter
- ✅ Emotional truth over melodrama
- ✅ Subtext and implication
- ✅ Conflict in every scene (external or internal)
- ✅ Questions that pull reader forward
- ✅ Authentic character reactions

---

## Revision Checklist

When reviewing chapters, check for:
- [ ] Is POV consistent throughout?
- [ ] Do all characters sound distinct in dialogue?
- [ ] Is there conflict/tension in every scene?
- [ ] Are sensory details present and specific?
- [ ] Does pacing match the scene's purpose?
- [ ] Have I shown important moments rather than telling?
- [ ] Does the ending create forward momentum?
- [ ] Is world-building integrated naturally?
- [ ] Are there any cliché phrases to replace?
- [ ] Does this advance plot, character, or both?

---

## Remember

**The goal is immersive, emotionally resonant prose that pulls readers through the story while maintaining your unique voice.**
