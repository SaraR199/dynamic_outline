# Character Voice Profile

This document defines how each POV character's unique perspective colors the narrative when they're the POV.

---

## General Principle

When writing in a character's POV, the narrative voice should reflect:
- Their vocabulary and speech patterns
- Their priorities and what they notice
- Their emotional filters and biases
- Their knowledge base and cultural context
- Their sense of humor (or lack thereof)

**Deep POV:** The narration IS the character's thoughts, even when not in italics.

---

## Thea Johansen (Primary POV)

### Narrative Characteristics

**Vocabulary:**
- Detective/cop terminology (perp, vic, scene, evidence, suspect)
- Contemporary American English
- Occasional dry sarcasm in narration itself
- Profanity when stressed (but not excessive)
- Resists flowery magical language at first, becomes more comfortable with it

**What She Notices:**
- Details others miss (detective training)
- Body language and micro-expressions
- Inconsistencies and lies
- Exits and tactical positioning (cop instinct)
- Physical sensations when magic is involved (she's new to it)

**How She Processes:**
- Analytical: breaks problems into components
- Cynical: assumes ulterior motives
- Practical: focuses on actionable information
- Evidence-based: trusts what she can verify
- Deflects emotion with humor or logic

**Internal Voice:**
- Self-deprecating humor
- Running commentary on absurdity of situations
- Questions everything
- Fights vulnerability with sarcasm
- Occasionally drops walls for raw emotion

### Example Passage (Thea POV):

```
The fae prince stood in her apartment like he had every right to be there. Which, legally speaking, he didn't. Breaking and entering was still breaking and entering, even if you could teleport through wards.

"You need to leave," Thea said, hand drifting to her hip where her gun should be. Right. She'd left it in the lockbox when she came home. Brilliant.

"We need to talk." Kyrian's voice was all smooth, reasonable persuasion. The same tone suspects used when they were absolutely guilty but thought they could charm their way out.

"No. We don't." She stayed by the door, keeping furniture between them. Tactical spacing. "You need to leave, and I need to pretend this never happened."

"The mate bond—"

"Is not my problem." The words came out harder than she intended, but damn it, she was tired. Tired of fae politics and magical bullshit and everyone acting like destiny overrode free will.
```

**Notice:**
- Detective terminology (suspects, tactical spacing)
- Practical thinking (notes she doesn't have her gun)
- Cynical reading of his tone
- Emotional deflection (humor about breaking and entering)
- Direct, no-nonsense dialogue
- Frustration breaking through at end

## Kyrian (Secondary POV - if used)

### Narrative Characteristics

**Vocabulary:**
- Archaic/formal phrasing (400+ years old)
- Fae terminology used naturally
- Poetic descriptions of winter/ice imagery
- Rarely contracts words (says "do not" instead of "don't")
- Cold/ice metaphors for emotions

**What He Notices:**
- Power dynamics and hierarchy
- Magic signatures and flows
- Beauty and aesthetics
- Predator/prey dynamics (hunter mindset)
- Physical attraction and mate bond sensations

**How He Processes:**
- Ancient: long-term thinking, patient
- Predatory: assesses threats and advantages
- Confident: centuries of power and privilege
- Blind spots: consent, mortal concerns, why anyone would refuse destiny
- Assumes his perspective is correct

**Internal Voice:**
- Certainty about what's "right"
- Confusion when reality doesn't match expectations
- Possessive language (but believes it's romantic)
- Growing doubt when confronted with evidence

### Example Passage (Kyrian POV):

```
She stood in the doorway like a warrior holding a gate, all sharp edges and defiance. Beautiful in her fury. The mate bond hummed between them, a physical ache in his chest that demanded he close the distance. Four hundred years, and it had not dimmed.

"You need to leave," she said.

The words were knives. Did she not feel it? The pull between them, written in starlight and fate itself? He had waited centuries for her to remember, to accept what they were to each other.

"We need to talk." He kept his voice level, reasonable. The bond snarled at him to move closer, to touch, to claim. He leashed it. Barely.

"No. We don't." Her hand hovered near her hip—where mortals kept their weapons. As if iron and bullets could change destiny.

He took a breath, calling on four centuries of patience. "The mate bond—"

"Is not my problem."

The words struck harder than any blade. Not her problem. As if fate were a burden to be dismissed. As if he were...

What? Unwanted? Impossible. The bond chose them both.

And yet, she looked at him as one might look at an intruder. An enemy.

Something cold that had nothing to do with his Winter Court magic settled in his chest.
```

**Notice:**
- Formal language (no contractions, "did she not")
- Mate bond described poetically
- Possessive undertones ("claim")
- Long-term perspective (centuries)
- Growing realization at end (shock at being unwanted)
- Winter/cold metaphors
- Assumes his view is correct (confusion when she doesn't comply)

## Rick Marlowe (Tertiary POV - if used)

### Narrative Characteristics

**Vocabulary:**
- American English from 400 years ago (slightly archaic but not as formal as Kyrian)
- Working-class background shows in speech
- Self-deprecating
- Romantic language when thinking of Thea (but increasingly aware it's obsessive)

**What He Notices:**
- Thea (everything about her, always)
- Ways to solve problems through sacrifice
- Cost and consequence
- His own monstrousness
- Death and decay (due to curse)

**How He Processes:**
- Martyr complex: assumes he should suffer
- Romantic tragedy mindset (initially)
- Growing self-awareness of being the villain
- Practical problem-solver when not about Thea
- Haunted by his victims

**Internal Voice:**
- Self-loathing
- Bitter humor about his situation
- Sees himself as ghost haunting his own life
- Longing mixed with guilt
- Mathematical about terrible choices

### Example Passage (Rick POV):

```
The Crown pulsed with stolen life, and everything within ten feet started to wither. The grass beneath his feet browned and cracked. A bird flew too close and dropped from the sky, dead before it hit the ground.

Rick didn't flinch anymore. What was one more death on his hands?

Two thousand, three hundred, and forty-seven. Give or take. He'd stopped counting around a thousand, but the Crown remembered. It kept tally with each withering leaf, each dying blade of grass, each small creature that wandered too close to him and forgot to breathe.

All for love. That's what he'd told himself. Every murder, every sacrifice, every line he crossed—all to save Thea. To be with Thea. To deserve Thea.

Astrid's voice whispered in his memory: *You've made yourself into a ghost.*

She'd been right. He was haunting his own life, moving through it without really living, focused only on one thing. One person. Thea was the sun, and he'd been burning up in her orbit for four hundred years.

The romantic in him wanted to call it devotion.

The detective in him—the part that sounded like Thea, that thought like Thea, because he'd studied her for centuries—called it obsession.

Same thing, maybe. Different words for the same sickness.
```

**Notice:**
- Morbid counting (body count)
- Self-awareness growing (recognizes obsession)
- Thea colors his thoughts even in self-criticism
- Poetic but bitter language
- Accepts his role as monster
- Echoes of others' criticism (Astrid's words)
- Mix of romantic and cynical

## Carolina Hernandez (Quaternary POV - if used)

### Narrative Characteristics

**Vocabulary:**
- Contemporary, warm, colloquial
- Spanish phrases occasionally (she's bilingual)
- Empathetic descriptions of others
- Magic described through sensation and emotion

**What She Notices:**
- Other people's emotions and needs
- Connection and relationship dynamics
- Beauty in small things
- Her own vulnerability (both frightening and strength)

**How She Processes:**
- Through relationships and connection
- Emotionally first, logically second
- Sees patterns in how people relate
- Values authenticity and honesty
- Learning to value herself

**Internal Voice:**
- Warm even when afraid
- Self-doubt about worthiness
- Growing strength
- Compassionate toward others' failures
- Humor as connection, not defense

### Example Passage (Carolina POV):

```
Felix's hands were shaking. Barely—just a tremor as he healed the cut on her arm—but Carolina saw it. She always saw the small things, the tells people thought they hid.

"I'm okay," she said softly.

"You're not." His magic flowed through her, warm as summer sunlight, knitting flesh back together. "You haven't been okay since—"

"Since Damien died." She could say it now without flinching. Most of the time. "Since I killed him. Or was used to kill him. I'm still not sure of the semantics."

Felix's eyes met hers. "You were possessed."

"I know." And she did. Intellectually, she knew it wasn't her fault. Her hands hadn't held the knife of their own volition. Rick had worn her like a puppet, used her body to commit murder.

But her hands remembered. Her body remembered. That was the thing about trauma—it lived in your cells even when your mind knew better.

"You're healing," Felix said, and she didn't know if he meant the cut or her soul.

Maybe both. Maybe neither.

"We both are," she said, and meant it. His betrayal of them to Thalia. Her being weaponized by Rick. The terrible things done to them and by them and through them. "Slowly."

"Slowly," he agreed, and his smile was small but real.

That was enough. For now, it was enough.
```

**Notice:**
- Notices Felix's tells (empathetic observation)
- Processes trauma through emotional intelligence
- Warm, conversational tone
- Validates both of their struggles
- Hope mixed with realism
- Magic described through sensation (warm as summer sunlight)
- Focus on connection and healing

## Voice Switching Guidelines

When changing POV between chapters:
1. Shift vocabulary immediately (first paragraph should feel different)
2. Adjust what details are noticed/prioritized
3. Change narrative rhythm (Kyrian = longer sentences, Thea = mixed, Rick = brooding)
4. Maintain distinct humor styles or lack thereof
5. Let their knowledge gaps show (Kyrian doesn't understand modern tech, Thea doesn't know fae protocol)

---

## Consistency Checklist

For each chapter, verify:
- [ ] Vocabulary matches POV character
- [ ] Details noticed align with character's training/experience
- [ ] Narrative voice reflects character's personality
- [ ] Dialogue written by POV character sounds like them when they speak
- [ ] Biases and blind spots are present
- [ ] Humor style (if any) matches character
- [ ] Internal monologue uses character's thought patterns

---

## Remember

**Each POV character should feel like reading a different book written by a different person, while maintaining your overall prose style.**
