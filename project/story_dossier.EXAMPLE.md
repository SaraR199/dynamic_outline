## project_essentials

### genre_and_subgenres
- Primary: Urban Fantasy
- Secondary: Detective Thriller
- Tertiary: Romantic Elements

### tone
Dark, gritty, with moments of unexpected hope. Think noir detective meets faerie politics.

### humor
Dry wit and gallows humor. Protagonist uses sarcasm as defense mechanism.

### primary_theme
The cost of power and the illusion of choice vs. true agency

### reader_experience_goal
Readers should feel the tension between destiny and free will, question whether choices matter when powerful forces manipulate from the shadows, and ultimately feel catharsis when the protagonist breaks the rules of fate itself.

## world_building

### key_world_rules

1. **The Veil:** Separation between mortal world and fae realm. Most humans can't perceive fae.

2. **Mate Bonds:** Fae magical connection that snaps between compatible individuals. Causes physical/emotional pull. Traditionally viewed as destiny, but story challenges this.

3. **The Four Courts:** Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter - each ruled by an Eldest (ancient fae)

4. **Signatory System:** Ancient magical contract binding certain fae to maintain the prison holding Korvain (the First Hunger)

5. **Time Differential:** Time moves differently in fae realm. Years there might be days in mortal world.

### key_world_details

**Chicago (Mortal World):**
- Setting for much of the action
- Has hidden fae population (changelings, half-bloods)
- Certain locations are thin spots where Veil is weak

**Fae Realm:**
- Four distinct court territories (Spring meadows, Summer deserts, Autumn forests, Winter tundra)
- Capital: The Crossroads where all courts meet
- Ancient sites of power scattered throughout

**Magic System:**
- Fae magic tied to nature and emotions
- Each court has elemental affinity
- Magic requires intention and will
- Costs: Using too much drains life force

**The Prison:**
- Ancient construct holding Korvain (entity of pure hunger)
- Maintained by Signatory magic and four divine artifacts
- Failing due to events in previous book
- If it breaks, both worlds at risk

## cultural_context

### Fae Court Politics

- Courts historically compete for power
- Eldest are viewed as near-gods
- Challenging an Eldest is death sentence
- Mate bonds are considered sacred/unquestionable
- Human life viewed as brief/insignificant by most fae

### Mortal World Integration

- Most humans unaware of fae
- Those who know either work with fae or are victims
- Changelings (human/fae mixed) live between worlds
- Some human institutions know and cooperate (ambiguous whether good or bad)
